European electricity-price forecasting: experiment record

This is the current consolidated account of the completed experiments, including negative results and rejected runs. It replaces scattered summaries as the starting point for reading results. Detailed protocols, original metrics and milestone snapshots remain linked for reproduction. Tables are rebuilt from saved results; no training or model selection occurs during report generation.

What we have learned

Recent auction prices explain most of the initial improvement over weekly persistence. Once models share strong recent-price inputs and pool training across markets, XGBoost, MLP and static GAT are much closer than the first per-zone comparison suggested. Graph improvements are modest and vary by market.

Our original GRU experiments need correction: their saved scores are reproducible, but the fused ROCm GRU disagrees with CPU on shuffled training-shaped batches. Those scores cannot establish that temporal learning is worse. The newer partner-inspired GRU uses a verified native GPU path and achieves about 21 EUR/MWh test MAE, close to strong static models. Its recipe differs in several ways, so the difference cannot be attributed to one change.

Weather and constraint results are mixed. We have retrospective ERA5-lineage weather and airport observations, not historical operational weather forecasts. JAO constraints entered as flat summaries; no completed experiment used CNECs as extra graph nodes. None of these results certifies a production-time noon forecast.

Data, forecast contract and metrics

At 12:00 Europe/Brussels on D−1, predict each hourly price of delivery day D. Origins use the previous local calendar date at literal noon, then convert to UTC. DST days retain 23 or 25 delivery hours. Prices are EUR/MWh: a prediction error of 21 EUR/MWh means a 21-euro price error per megawatt-hour of energy.

The canonical master has 94,967 UTC hours, 62 historical market identities and 5,887,954 node rows, January 2016–October 2026. It preserves missingness, negative prices and DE_AT_LU separately from DE_LU. Forty-nine identities have labels in every required partition and form the later evaluation cohort. Targets are never filled and missing weather never removes target rows.

Partition, Brussels delivery date Dates Purpose
Pretrain + train January 2016–March 2024 Fit models and learned preprocessing
Validation April 2024–March 2025 Early stopping and validation diagnostics
Test April 2025–September 2026 Previously explored evaluation period
Unassigned October 2026 Coverage/structural audit only

MAE is mean absolute price error, not classification accuracy. RMSE weights large errors more strongly. Primary all-target evaluation uses 428,427 validation and 634,578 test labels. The weekly comparison additionally requires history: 428,213 validation and 633,885 test labels. Paired MAE and all-target MAE are different populations. Multi-seed entries average scores, not predictions.

Weekly persistence copies the same market's price exactly 168 elapsed hours earlier. Relative MAE is model MAE divided by persistence MAE on those same hours; 0.72 means 28% lower error. Percentage error relative to the actual price is unstable around zero and negative prices, so we use persistence-relative MAE. Seed SD measures fit variability; validation-week bootstrap intervals condition on the fitted models and are not comprehensive uncertainty estimates.

ENTSO-E prices/load/flows are pinned to v1.2. The supplied weather export is ERA5-lineage; its requested v1.1 release identity remains unresolved. Original CWE/CORE JAO exports retain generation and file hashes. Current data limitations and the price-publication correction are in the leakage audit and pipeline comparison. An observation's timestamp is not its publication time. All conditional columns remain conditional here.

Our GRUs versus the partner-inspired GRU

Our original GRU+MLP and GRU+GAT were actually trained across five seeds. They were not placeholders. Their archived paired test MAEs are 24.4997 and 23.5875. The November 9 audit now classifies both families as affected historical runs.

The base fallback in the new GRU reconstructs predictions only: weekly price, else guarded daily price, else zero. It never fills a missing target. Both recipes use training-only preprocessing and preserve historical zone identities. Its net inflow is a completeness-checked proxy over training graph pairs, not a claimed national net-import total. Its spread objective couples zones during training; the MLP makes no inference-time graph messages.

Backend interpretation: saved predictions can replay correctly while the training calculation differs from the intended GRU. Our small consecutive inference batches passed, but the shuffled training-input batches failed the fused path. Native GPU matched CPU. This audit runs forward passes with dropout disabled; it does not inspect every batch or verify backward derivatives. We have not retrained the original ten models. Their old scores remain available for provenance and cannot establish a temporal-architecture disadvantage.

Likewise, the first partner-style fused run is rejected. Its corrected native run passed CPU checks and full saved-prediction verification. Switching an old checkpoint to native inference alone would not correct its training history. Compare the original-family audit and partner-family audit.

The accepted residual GRU has all-target test MAE 21.0677 and RMSE 47.0222. It is close to static XGBoost/GAT on MAE but worse on RMSE; its validation MAE is lower. One seed and several simultaneous recipe changes prevent causal claims about the residual target, weather, spread objective or GRU architecture.

Airport temperature: all nine variants

One airport per country is selected by 2023 passenger volume. Regional zones share that country's station; combined-market proxies and unresolved mappings are explicit. Latest temperature must be at least two and at most six hours old at origin. The optional 24-hour mean requires 18 sampled hours. These features are frozen across the delivery day. Models use recent prices/calendar and airport features, without ERA5, load, JAO or realised-flow predictors.

Gains are small and inconsistent across markets. Latest-temperature validation intervals include zero for XGBoost and MLP; GAT's interval is narrowly positive, unadjusted for multiple comparisons. About 87.3% of pooled XGBoost's headline test improvement comes from an unmapped market whose airport features are all missing. Joint fitting and missing-value routes can change its predictions; that improvement cannot be credited to a local temperature reading. France worsens for all three models. Original receipt/revision times are absent, so this is still conditional observation evidence, not a proven as-of feed.

France temperature and pipeline investigations

These temperature diagnostics extend the original weekly/raw-price France model, not the later rich/pooled models. The current-origin proxy is one completed ERA5-lineage temperature reading frozen across day D. The perfect temperature case deliberately includes the realised target-hour temperature and is labelled an oracle, excluded from forecasting rankings.

Neither temperature addition beats France persistence's 32.4425 test MAE. This does not imply that operational regional weather forecasts are unhelpful.

The partner XGBoost reproduction scored 21.2539 test MAE across 43 legacy zones, close to its published 21.2433. It scored 22.0893 on France. It uses a richer feature set, legacy geography/fills and different tree parameters. It cannot be ranked against a 49-market or ten-market aggregate as if the populations match. The partner's published GRU+MLP score of 20.4014 belongs to that separate protocol; we adapted its method rather than reproducing that numerical result. The separate legacy persistence artifact scores 30.1463 validation and 29.0087 test MAE over 376,680/565,320 zone-hours. It is historical context on that legacy cohort, not a replacement for the canonical persistence comparator of 29.1334.

What remains usable, and what comes next

Evidence Current interpretation
Static XGBoost/MLP/GAT and airport comparisons Verified retrospective scores; conditional availability and repeated-test limits apply

CNEC/heterogeneous constraint-node configurations Planned scaffold, no completed fit
France/synthetic smoke runs Correctness gates, not scientific performance estimates

The next controlled temporal test should rerun our original GRU+MLP and GRU+GAT recipes with the verified native backend, the same seeds and masks. Separately, repeat the residual recipe across seeds and ablate histories, residual target and contrast loss one change at a time. These runs have not been launched as part of documentation cleanup.

For production work, preserve daily issue-time snapshots, record predictions before outcomes arrive, compare against weekly persistence and strong pooled static baselines, and monitor coverage and inference reliability. This would measure prospective performance without reusing the explored test period as a new confirmation set. Operational temperature requires an as-of observation or forecast archive; retrospective ERA5 is not such an archive.
