\begin{abstract}
\ifdefined\ControlledStudyComplete
\ControlledAbstract
\else
We build an auditable European electricity-price dataset and evaluate three
XGBoost variants and real/shuffled graph attention models across 49 market
identities. Real-graph GAT achieves \SuiteGraphTest\ EUR/MWh test MAE, improving
\SuiteShuffleGain\% over its shuffled control and \SuiteXgbGain\% over recent-price
XGBoost. Results are exploratory and conditional on unverified historical
publication vintages.
\fi
\end{abstract}

\section{Approach}
At 12:00 Europe/Brussels on calendar day $D-1$, predict every hourly day-ahead
price of delivery day $D$, including 23/25-hour daylight-saving days. We implement
an independent dataset builder, per-zone XGBoost \cite{chen}, and pooled hourly
GATv2 models \cite{gat}. Directed graph edges are physical pairs observed during
training; historical node lifetimes mask inactive identities. A shuffled control
rewires non-self edges, preserving static in/out degrees without duplicates.
Both GATs add self-loops, testing real topology under the same neural learner.

\section{Experiments}
\paragraph{Exact data and partitions.}
The master contains 5,887,954 zone-hours for 62 price-area identities, January
2016--October 2026. ENTSO-E prices, load forecasts and physical flows use releases
v1.2. Additional sources are PECD/ERA5 temperature, anomaly and wind/solar capacity
factors (2010--September 2026), and original CWE/CORE JAO exports
(May 2015--October 2026). Weather is reanalysis, not archived forecasts; its
requested v1.1 release identity is unverified. Missing values and negative prices
are retained, and DE\_AT\_LU remains separate from DE\_LU. Forty-nine identities
have valid targets in fit, validation and test; the other 13 remain explicitly
unscored, while all 62 can provide dated graph context.

Fit on January 1, 2016--March 31, 2024 (pretrain + train); validate on April 1,
2024--March 31, 2025; test on April 1, 2025--September 30, 2026, using Brussels
delivery dates. October stays unassigned. MAE and RMSE are measured after inverse
transformation, in EUR/MWh. Weekly persistence copies the same identity's price
168 elapsed hours earlier. Targets are never filled or filtered for feature
completeness; neural preprocessing is fitted on training-period features only.

\ifdefined\ControlledStudyComplete
\ControlledMethods
\else
\paragraph{Five models.}
Recent-price XGBoost uses local hour, weekday, month and guarded 24/168-hour
price lags. Richer XGBoost adds load forecast, four realised weather features
at lag 48, and three lag-48 JAO summaries: presolved constraint count, minimum
RAM ratio and domain presence. Neighbour XGBoost adds incoming neighbours'
recent-price means and finite-value counts. Both GATs use the richer node inputs;
they share parameters across zones, whereas XGBoost fits per zone. Missing inputs
remain NaN for trees; neural models use training medians/scaling and missingness
indicators. No same-day realised flow or target price is supplied as a predictor.

All models fit $z=\operatorname{asinh}(p/50)$ with L1 loss and invert predictions
as $50\sinh(\hat z)$. XGBoost uses depth 6, at most 600 trees, learning rate 0.05,
row/column sampling 0.8 and 50-round validation patience. GAT uses two layers,
64 hidden channels, four heads, dropout 0.1, Adam at 0.001, batch size 64,
at most 30 epochs and patience five. Seed is 224; stopping uses transformed
validation MAE. CPU XGBoost and ROCm GAT runs completed in about six minutes total.

Real-graph GAT improves test MAE by \SuiteShuffleGain\% over shuffled GAT,
\SuiteXgbGain\% over recent-price XGBoost, and \SuitePersistenceGain\% over
persistence. The richer feature bundle does not improve aggregate XGBoost test
MAE; neighbour inputs improve validation but nearly match the recent-price test
baseline. These group additions do not isolate individual weather/load/JAO effects.
The full target population, including hours without persistence, has
\SuiteAllValHours\ validation and \SuiteAllTestHours\ test hours. On all test
targets, real GAT RMSE is \SuiteGraphRmse\ versus \SuiteRecentRmse\ for recent-price
XGBoost. Real GAT selects epoch 28 of 30; shuffled GAT selects epoch 21 and
stops at 26. Both real and shuffled runs use about 0.185 GiB peak tensor allocation,
excluding ROCm context. All saved predictions have unique keys and matching target
masks; independent recomputation agrees with reported MAE.

\paragraph{Earlier pipeline diagnostics.}
We reproduced a teammate's richer XGBoost pipeline at commit \texttt{757da793}:
43-zone test MAE 21.25 versus published 21.24. Its legacy table, geography and
inputs differ, so this is a separate cohort from Table~\ref{tab:suite}. In a fixed
France learner, adding prior-day auction prices reduces test MAE from
\WeeklyCalendarTest\ to \RecentPriceTest\ (30.8\%). Validation improvement is
11.16 EUR/MWh (95\% paired calendar-week bootstrap interval
[\PriceBootstrapLower, \PriceBootstrapUpper], 2,000 resamples conditional on the
fitted models). Masking post-origin realised weather gives \PastWeatherTest\ test
MAE versus \AllWeatherTest\ when supplied; such weather does not explain most
of that France gain. These diagnostics motivated the stronger recent-price baseline.

\paragraph{Timing and limitations.}
Previous-day auction prices are normally published on $D-2$ \cite{auction};
delivery time is not publication time. We guard elapsed lag 24 against reaching
the target delivery date on the autumn transition. Weather, load and JAO historical
revision/publication vintages remain unverified: lag 48 alone cannot establish
availability, especially for retrospective ERA5 \cite{era5}. Scores are conditional
benchmarks, not certified operational forecasts. Training-period topology is static;
dated lifetime masks do not reconstruct every commissioning date. One seed and
previous exploratory test inspection prevent a confirmatory significance claim.
Real/shuffled GAT is the matched topology control; GAT versus per-zone XGBoost
also changes parameter sharing. We have not evaluated detailed constraint nodes.

\section{Future work}
Repeat across seeds and assess paired loss uncertainty; compare pooled tabular
models to separate topology gains from parameter sharing. Obtain archived weather
forecasts and publication-aware load/JAO inputs. Then evaluate temporal encoders
and detailed constraint nodes against the established recent-price and graph
baselines, keeping physical flows, scheduled exchanges and MaxBex distinct.
