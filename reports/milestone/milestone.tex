\begin{abstract}
We construct an auditable European electricity-price dataset and reproduce a
teammate's XGBoost pipeline. Controlled France experiments identify prior-day
auction prices as the largest measured feature improvement; post-origin realised
weather adds little. These conditional benchmarks establish a stronger baseline
for subsequent graph experiments; historical publication vintages remain unverified.
\end{abstract}

\section{Approach}
At 12:00 Europe/Brussels on calendar day $D-1$, predict every hourly day-ahead
price of delivery day $D$, including 23/25-hour daylight-saving days. Our planned
graph connects bidding zones, interconnectors and JAO transmission constraints.
We implement a dataset builder and per-zone XGBoost \cite{chen}, and reproduce
the teammate's \texttt{c1\_xgb\_zone} pipeline unchanged at \texttt{757da793}.

\section{Experiments}
\paragraph{Exact data.}
Our master has 5,887,954 zone-hours, 62 price-area identities, January 2016--October
2026. ENTSO-E prices, load forecasts and physical flows use releases v1.2.
We join PECD/ERA5 temperature, anomaly and wind/solar capacity factors
(2010--September 2026) and original CWE/CORE JAO exports (May 2015--October 2026).
Weather is reanalysis; its v1.1 release identity is unverified. We retain NaNs,
negative prices and separate DE\_AT\_LU/DE\_LU identities. The partner's original
v1.0 table has 4,240,035 rows and 45 zones through September 2026, with filled
inputs and a historical German splice. The appendix records hashes and geography.

\paragraph{Splits and metrics.}
Fit on January 1, 2016--March 31, 2024 (pretrain + train); validate on April 1,
2024--March 31, 2025; test on April 1, 2025--September 30, 2026, using Brussels
delivery dates. October 2026 remains unassigned. MAE and RMSE are in EUR/MWh.
Weekly persistence copies the same zone's price 168 elapsed hours earlier.
Comparisons use identical valid target-hour masks, never feature-complete subsets
or filled labels. The ten zones BE, FR, NL, DE\_LU, AT, ES, PT, PL, CZ, CH supply
87,600 validation and 131,520 test zone-hours per method.

\paragraph{Models and aggregate results.}
Our inputs are hour, weekday, month, UTC offset, CWE/CORE era flags and weekly
price. Switching raw-price squared error to $z=\operatorname{asinh}(p/50)$ with
L1 loss improves test MAE; invert with $50\sinh(\hat z)$ before scoring.
The partner adds prior-day price, load forecast, four weather lags and net imports,
and changes tree settings (Table~\ref{tab:matched}). Its full 43-zone reproduction
scores 21.25 test MAE versus the published 21.24.

\paragraph{Controlled France experiments.}
Fix the partner learner: CPU histogram trees, depth 6, at most 600 rounds,
learning rate 0.05, row/column sampling 0.8, L1 objective, asinh scale 50,
seed 0, and 50-round early stopping on transformed validation MAE. Retain all
72,310 training, 8,760 validation and 13,152 test targets.

Changing only our seven-feature model to partner hyperparameters worsens test
MAE to 36.32. Its equivalent legacy-table control matches within $10^{-6}$
EUR/MWh. Removing the era/offset inputs and adopting the partner feature order gives
\WeeklyCalendarTest. Adding prior-day auction prices then reduces test MAE to
\RecentPriceTest\ (30.8\%); the validation improvement is 11.16 EUR/MWh
(95\% paired calendar-week bootstrap interval [\PriceBootstrapLower,
\PriceBootstrapUpper], 2,000 replicates). This is the largest measured addition;
other increments are small and mixed.

\paragraph{Timing audit and interpretation.}
Previous-day day-ahead prices are normally auctioned on $D-2$ \cite{auction};
their delivery time does not imply unavailability. However, an elapsed 24-hour lag
can reach day $D$ on the 25-hour autumn transition; we mask that case (one
validation and one test hour). Half the original weather-lag-24 observations occur
after the origin. Masking them gives \PastWeatherTest\ test MAE, versus
\AllWeatherTest\ when supplied; using 48-hour weather lags gives
\WeatherFortyEightTest. Post-origin weather therefore does not explain most of
the France advantage. Past effective time still cannot prove publication availability:
ERA5 is retrospective \cite{era5}, and load/weather revision vintages are absent.
These are conditional scores, not certified operational forecasts.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
This one-country, one-seed sequence is order-dependent, not causal feature
importance. Bootstrap intervals condition on fitted models. Repeated exploratory
test inspection prevents a fresh blind-confirmation claim. Detailed CNEC and
graph models remain future work.

\section{Future work}
Establish a publication-aware recent-price baseline, obtain archived weather
forecasts, and repeat across countries and seeds. Compare neighbour features,
interconnector graphs and JAO constraint nodes under identical evaluation rules,
keeping physical flows, scheduled exchanges and MaxBex distinct.
